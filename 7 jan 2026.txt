1. Zero-Shot Prompting

Meaning:
The model is asked to do a task without any examples.

Example:

“Translate this sentence into English:
नमस्ते”

Why it’s called zero-shot:

Zero examples are given

Model uses its pre-training knowledge

When to use:

Simple tasks

Common problems

Exam line:

Zero-shot prompting means giving a task to the model without providing any examples.

->2. Few-Shot Prompting

Meaning:
The model is given a few examples before the actual task.

Example:

Text: “I love this movie.” → Positive
Text: “This is a bad product.” → Negative

Now classify:
“The service was okay.”

Why it’s useful:

Improves accuracy

Helps model understand pattern

When to use:

Classification

Formatting tasks

Exam line:

Few-shot prompting provides a small number of examples to guide the model’s output.

->3. Chain-of-Thought Prompting

Meaning:
The model is encouraged to think step-by-step before answering.

Example:

“Solve this problem step by step:
If a pen costs ₹10, how much do 5 pens cost?”

Output:

One pen costs ₹10
5 pens = 10 × 5 = ₹50

Why it’s powerful:

Improves reasoning

Reduces mistakes

When to use:

Math

Logic

Complex reasoning

Exam line:

Chain-of-Thought prompting asks the model to explain intermediate reasoning steps.
